Counter-Attacking

Pro Coach Series — Wes Hughes, Wolves U15/U16  ·  adapted for Arrow Soccer

  • Age U12–U16
  • Duration ~60 min
  • Phases 3
  • Theme Win it, break out, finish
1Breakout Game 2Wave Game 3Directional Game

One pitch, three phases

Set the area up once and run all three phases through it. Long, narrow pitch between the two 18-yard boxes, split into thirds. Goalkeepers in both goals.

18m end third  ·  15m central third  ·  18m end third  ·  20m wide

Part 1

Breakout Game

Possession v pressing, then break out 2v1

How it runs

  1. Two teams of four start in the middle third with three neutrals — one in the middle third, one on each edge of it.
  2. The coach plays into the possession team, Team A, who try to string 10 consecutive passes for a point.
  3. The three neutrals support Team A for an overload. The end neutrals are fixed and become defenders if Team B steals it; the middle neutral moves anywhere in the central area.
  4. Team B press to steal. On the win they break out of the central area and attack 2v1 against the nearest neutral, finishing in the nearest goal.
  5. Steal and score → the coach feeds a new ball to their remaining players in the central zone to attack the other goal.
  6. Ball out, or the press fails to score → the coach feeds the possession team again and play continues.
  7. Swap roles after each three-minute working block.

In possession

  • ComposureQuality passing and receiving under aggressive pressure.
  • Quick combination playOne and two touch keeps the pressing team from stealing it.
  • Disguised passingDisguise reduces the defenders’ ability to read play and intercept.

Out of possession

  • Protect central areasThe pressing team forces play into wide areas where space is limited.
  • Pressing triggersPoor or miscontrolled passes, slow ball speed.
  • Press as a teamThe four hunt the ball together — restricts space and applies maximum pressure.
  • “Smother or recover”Recognise when to press and when to drop and regain shape.
  • Attacking transitionKnow when to drive at the defender 1v1 and when to combine for the 2v1 — at match tempo.
Part 2

Attacking / Defending Wave Game

Three teams of three, 4v3 every attack

How it runs

  1. Team A start in the central third; Teams B and D start next to the goal in their end thirds.
  2. A neutral is locked in each end third and plays for whichever team is attacking there.
  3. The coach plays into Team A, who attack either goal. As soon as they commit, the team beside that goal gets out fast to defend.
  4. Team A plus the neutral attack with a 4v3 overload and look to score.
  5. Attack over → Team A become the resting defenders. Team B take a new ball and attack the opposite goal against Team D.
  6. Team B then rest, and Team D attack the far end against Team A. The rotation continues for a set working period.
  7. If the defending team win it, they counter as quickly as possible. The attacking team may recover and win it back until the halfway line — after that they must let the counter go.

In possession

  • “Run hard with the ball”Drive at goal and force the defenders to decide — hold or press.
  • Forward runs off the ballRuns beyond the first defender create through balls, or drag defenders out as decoys.
  • Individual attackingEven with the overload, use tricks, skills and space to attack 1v1 when the space is there.
  • Create space to shoot1v1 or quick combination — find the shooting chance as often as possible.

Out of possession

  • Limit space by halving the pitchForce the ball wide and get numbers around it to steal.
  • Cover & balanceAs the first defender presses, second and third cover to kill the forward pass — talk throughout.
  • Delay or stealWhen underloaded, recognise when to go and win it and when to hold, delay and force outside.
Part 3

Themed Directional Game

6v6, receive centrally and counter fast

How it runs

  1. Two teams of six plus goalkeepers. All 12 outfield players start in the central third.
  2. The coach always serves into the central third to begin. After the serve, no one is locked into a third.
  3. Once the first pass is made, three players from the team in possession attack their designated goal while three defenders drop into the defensive third.
  4. If a team scores, the coach feeds their remaining attackers in the central area. Everyone from the previous phase works back quickly.
  5. Ball out or no goal → the coach passes to the opposite team, who attack 3v3 the other way; the previous attackers and defenders make fast recovery runs back.
  6. Progression: add a fourth attacker — and a recovering fourth defender — as the practice develops.
  7. Offsides are in play throughout. Play for a set period at the coach’s discretion.

In possession

  • “Quick or careful”On the steal, recognise whether to counter into space or be patient and move the defence first.
  • Direct playMostly a final-third game — be direct on and off the ball, and stay creative.
  • Underlaps / overlapsEven in a narrow area, these runs cause confusion and create space.

Out of possession

  • Defending 1v1This game lends itself to wide 1v1s — body shape sends the attacker where you want him.
  • Recognise the chance to stealDelay and force wide first, then punish a heavy touch or slow ball speed.
  • Hold the lineOffsides are in play, so communicate and keep the defensive line level.
